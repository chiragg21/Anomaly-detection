# Anomaly Detection in NYC Taxi Ridership

A time-series anomaly detection project (MTH442 course project) on the New York City taxi dataset — identifying anomalous days in ridership and characterizing normal daily demand patterns using ARMA modeling.

## Dataset

The New York Taxi dataset: number of taxi passengers from July 2014 to January 2015, recorded in 30-minute intervals (10,320 time instances total), as two columns — timestamp and passenger count.

## Approach

**1. Anomaly detection**
- Consolidated the 30-minute data into daily intervals to detect anomalies on specific days.
- Removed trend and seasonality components from the series.
- Checked the series for covariance stationarity.
- Selected ARMA(p, q) orders and fit the model.
- Computed residuals (observed − predicted) and flagged anomalies at a 5% significance level.

**2. Peak-demand analysis**
- Removed the days flagged as anomalous.
- Averaged ridership across all 48 daily intervals, split by weekday vs. weekend.

## Findings

- 7 anomalies were found across 215 days, generally coinciding with a festival or a natural calamity.
- Weekday demand peaks 8am–3pm and 6pm–11pm; weekend demand peaks 10am–4pm and 6pm–1am.
- Weekdays have more daytime working-hour demand; weekends shift more demand into the night.
